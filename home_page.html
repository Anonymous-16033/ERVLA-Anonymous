Abstract

Embodied chain-of-thought (CoT) aims to connect reasoning with robot action, yet its effective form and integration remain underexplored. We revisit embodied CoT as reasoning pre-training for action control and show that useful CoT must link task understanding to concrete action guidance: high-level reasoning alone gives limited gains, while action-oriented signals such as movement and end-effector trajectories in 2D image space are more effective. We further identify CoT contamination, where dense but noisy grounding labels, such as unstable bounding boxes or drifting gripper coordinates, overwrite useful representations and harm control. Reasoning dropout mitigates this issue by making explicit CoT optional and encouraging reasoning to be internalized into action-relevant states. Based on these insights, we build the largest embodied CoT corpus to date, with 978,743 trajectories, 226.3M samples, and 2592.5 hours, and present ERVLA, a reasoning-capable VLA whose reasoning mode can be switched on or off. Unlike knowledge-insulated designs that block action gradients, ERVLA internalizes embodied reasoning as semantic guidance while allowing action losses to shape the VLM backbone, enabling fully end-to-end training. ERVLA achieves state-of-the-art results on LIBERO-Plus and VLABench, especially in out-of-distribution settings, with 86.9% average success on LIBERO-Plus and 53.2% average success on VLABench.

Method

As illustrated in Fig. 2, ERVLA integrates explicit embodied CoT pre-training with end-to-end continuous-action generation. The system is designed around three components: structured embodied CoT data construction, a cache-conditioned VLA architecture, and a training recipe that makes reasoning optional at inference while preserving its representational benefits.

Structured Embodied CoT Data

ERVLA uses a large-scale embodied CoT corpus as reasoning pre-training data. Instead of treating CoT as free-form explanations, the annotation pipeline decomposes reasoning into task understanding, spatial grounding, subgoal planning, and action-oriented motion description. The corpus is built from open VLA datasets including Bridge, Fractal, DROID, MolmoAct, and AgiBot, covering single-arm and dual-arm manipulation as well as single-view and multi-view observations.

Reasoning-Aware VLA Architecture

ERVLA instantiates the reasoning backbone with Qwen3-VL-4B and keeps explicit embodied CoT supervision in the standard language-modeling form. Trainable state, action-query, and score-query interfaces expose action-sensitive states inside the VLM. A choice branch predicts multiple candidate continuous action chunks and their scores, while a DiT-style diffusion policy generates the final continuous action chunk by attending to the VLM's per-layer key-value cache.

End-to-End Reasoning Dropout

Unlike knowledge-insulated VLA training, ERVLA allows continuous-action losses to update the VLM backbone. It combines token-level CoT supervision, rectified-flow action loss, choice supervision, score prediction, and knowledge truncation, where DiT conditions only on the semantic-prefix cache instead of synthetic control-query turns. Reasoning dropout converts each sample into either a CoT or no-CoT condition, reducing dependence on visible reasoning and making the reasoning mode switchable at test time.

Experiments

LIBERO-Plus Zero-Shot Transfer

We evaluate ERVLA on LIBERO-Plus, where models are post-trained on LIBERO and evaluated by zero-shot direct transfer under task-suite and perturbation shifts. ERVLA reaches a 86.9% total success rate, outperforming strong VLA baselines including OpenVLA-OFT, π0, π0-FAST, PokeVLA, and π0.5. The gains are especially clear under out-of-distribution conditions such as camera, robot, language, lighting, background, noise, and layout perturbations.

Real-World Evaluation

We also deploy ERVLA on physical robots using one third-person camera and one wrist camera. The real-world task suite includes placing items into drawers and clearing tabletop waste across four tiers: Basic, Distractors, Semantic, and Long-horizon. The main gap appears under semantic ambiguity, distractors, and long-horizon dependencies, where ERVLA benefits from internalized embodied reasoning and cleaner diffusion-policy conditioning.

Embodied CoT Analysis

We first study which embodied CoT fields are actually useful for action learning. Isolated high-level understanding fields such as goal, planning, subtask, and reasoning provide limited gains or even reduce performance when used alone. In contrast, action-oriented fields such as movement and point trajectories improve control more directly. The strongest results appear when semantic understanding is coupled with concrete action guidance, indicating that effective embodied CoT must bridge task intent and executable motion.

We further identify CoT contamination: dense but inaccurate grounded labels, especially unstable bounding boxes and drifting gripper coordinates, can overwrite useful representations and harm control. Reasoning dropout reduces this failure mode by making explicit CoT optional during training and encouraging the model to internalize reasoning into action-relevant hidden states.

Ablation Studies

We conduct ablation studies to evaluate how embodied CoT should interact with continuous-action policies. The results show that explicit CoT alone does not scale reliably when treated as an autoregressive prefix for action decoding. Instead, ERVLA improves when reasoning supervision and action losses jointly shape the VLM representation.

Removing the choice branch weakens action-aware representation shaping, while knowledge isolation blocks action gradients from updating the VLM backbone and limits reasoning-action co-adaptation. Removing knowledge truncation lets DiT attend to synthetic control-query tokens, which can introduce shortcut learning and reduce robustness.

The final ERVLA design combines explicit linguistic CoT, reasoning dropout, choice-based action shaping, knowledge truncation, and cache-conditioned diffusion. This enables embodied CoT to scale as representation supervision instead of becoming a brittle decoding burden.

Scaling and Real-World Transfer

ERVLA shows that embodied CoT can scale when it is used to shape representations rather than as a literal action prefix. With the choice branch, knowledge truncation, and end-to-end flow supervision, performance improves steadily as embodied CoT pre-training data increases on both LIBERO-Plus and VLABench.

In real-world experiments, ERVLA is trained on approximately 10 hours of demonstrations covering object pick-and-place, drawer opening and closing, and tabletop waste clearing. The evaluation spans Basic, Distractors, Semantic, and Long-horizon tiers, with five instructions per tier and five trials per instruction.

The largest gains appear under semantic ambiguity, distractors, and long-horizon dependencies. These results support the central conclusion of the paper: real-world robustness depends not only on stronger low-level action generation, but on whether reasoning supervision is converted into grounded, action-aware representations that remain useful under out-of-distribution semantic and temporal shifts.
